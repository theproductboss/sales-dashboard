# The Scorecard

Ten dimensions, each scored 1 to 5 with a quoted line as evidence, weighted to a 100-point call score. Anchors below define what a 1, 3, and 5 look like so scores stay consistent from call to call and reviewer to reviewer.

## Weights by call type

| # | Dimension | One-call close (high ticket) | Two-call / B2B discovery | Setter / triage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frame and control | 8 | 8 | 15 |
| 2 | Clarify the why | 8 | 12 | 20 |
| 3 | Pain depth | 16 | 18 | 15 |
| 4 | Outcome and gap | 10 | 12 | 10 |
| 5 | Cost of inaction and label | 8 | 10 | 5 |
| 6 | Pre-handle and pitch | 10 | 5 | 0 |
| 7 | Ask for the sale | 12 | 8 (ask for next step) | 15 (ask for the booking) |
| 8 | Objection handling | 14 | 10 | 10 |
| 9 | Talk ratio and listening | 8 | 12 | 5 |
| 10 | Tonality and certainty | 6 | 5 | 5 |
| | **Total** | **100** | **100** | **100** |

Call score = sum of (dimension score ÷ 5 × weight). House weights override these once the playbook amendments say so.

## Grades

- **90 to 100, A:** exemplar. Save it and index its best moments.
- **80 to 89, B:** solid. One or two fixable leaks.
- **65 to 79, C:** the call worked in spite of something, or died of one clear thing.
- **50 to 64, D:** a skipped stage. Usually discovery.
- **Below 50, F:** no framework was run. Coach the structure before anything else.

## Automatic flags (shown above the score, never averaged away)

- **Never asked for the sale.** Caps dimension 7 at 1 and is listed first in the fixes. Hormozi and Martell both name this as the number one way calls die.
- **Pitched before the prospect stated the pain.** Caps dimension 6 at 2.
- **Rep talk share over 65 percent** in the discovery segment.
- **A rep monologue over 2 minutes** before the pitch.
- **Rep filled the silence after the price** (spoke first within about 3 seconds).
- **Discount offered before an objection was isolated.**
- **Oversold after a yes** (added features or bonuses after agreement instead of going to paperwork).
- **Prospect signal ignored:** a non-committal phrase, a partner reference, or a cost-based comment showed up in discovery, the rep didn't act on it, and the matching objection came later. See `transcript-signals.md`.
- **Follow-up left open:** the call ended in "I'll send info" or "I'll think about it" with no next meeting booked on the call (Martell's book a meeting from a meeting).
- **Compliance or ethics risk:** income or result guarantees, invented scarcity, pressure past a clear no, misrepresenting the offer. These are flagged regardless of outcome and outrank everything else in the report.
- **Correct disqualification.** Positive flag: the rep identified a bad fit and ended cleanly. Don't score this call as a loss.

## Dimension anchors

**1. Frame and control.** Sets an agenda, gets permission to ask questions, owns the call's direction.
- 1: No agenda; the prospect drives; rep chases tangents.
- 3: Agenda stated but loose; control lost once or twice and recovered.
- 5: States why they're on the call before small talk (Sapp); clear upfront frame with permission to ask questions (Martell's doctor frame: "is it okay if I ask a few questions to see where you're at?"); redirects rants kindly; the prospect qualifies to the rep, not the reverse (Miner).

**2. Clarify the why.** Why they booked, why now, what they want.
- 1: Skipped, or took the first surface answer.
- 3: Got the goal but not why it matters or why now.
- 5: Got the goal, the personal reason behind it, and the trigger that made them book now, in the prospect's own words.

**3. Pain depth.** Past attempts, why they failed, what the problem is costing (money, time, identity, relationships).
- 1: No past attempts explored; the rep described the pain for them.
- 3: Past attempts listed but not why they failed; the cost stays abstract.
- 5: Ran the full pain cycle (Hormozi's Overview): every past attempt and why it failed; threaded several follow-ups off each pain point (Sapp); took the consequence several layers deep, from business impact down to personal and family impact (Miner); quantified the cost (Sapp's negative data); the prospect said something emotionally real out loud.

**4. Outcome and gap.** A specific, felt picture of the result.
- 1: No future state discussed.
- 3: A number or goal, but not pressure-tested or personal.
- 5: A specific 6 to 12 month picture, the goal pressure-tested ("why 50K and not 49?"), non-committal "at least" goals challenged and re-anchored (Miner), unrealistic goals brought back to earth (Sapp), what it would mean to them personally, and the gap between now and then made explicit (Martell).

**5. Cost of inaction and label.** What happens if nothing changes, why now, and restating the core problem with a yes before pitching.
- 1: No restatement.
- 3: A summary, but in the rep's language, or no confirmation asked.
- 5: The cost of inaction asked ("if nothing changes in six months, where are you?"); the prospect said in their own words why now (Sapp, Miner's push-away question); one clean restatement of the core problem, confirmed ("Yes, exactly"), without parroting every answer (Miner); a commitment test (Martell's 1 to 10 "now or later") before the pitch.

**6. Pre-handle and pitch.** Surfacing likely objections before the price, then presenting only what solves their stated pain.
- 1: Generic feature dump; no permission asked.
- 3: Mostly relevant but includes features the prospect never mentioned needing.
- 5: Likely objections (spouse, price, time) pre-handled in discovery without inventing new ones (Sapp, Martell); transitioned by tying the bow ("if we did A, B, and C, would that help?") plus a similar-client story; each pillar tied back to a pain the prospect named, in their words; short stories, not specs; simple ("sell the vacation, not the plane"); no free consulting.

**7. Ask for the sale.**
- 1: Never asked, or asked so vaguely it wasn't a question ("so, yeah, let me know").
- 3: Asked once, clearly, but folded at the first hesitation.
- 5: A non-money micro buy-in first (Sapp); the price stated plainly as an "investment" tied to their goal, then silence; an assumptive this-or-that ask, not "are you ready?" (Martell); clarifying questions answered without treating them as objections; asked again after each concern; on yes, straight to paperwork with no overselling, a lock-in question (Martell's takeaway close), and no silent paperwork.

**8. Objection handling.**
- 1: Argued, got defensive, dropped price immediately, or accepted the stall and hung up.
- 3: Acknowledged and responded, but didn't isolate the real objection or didn't re-close.
- 5: Acknowledge without agreeing ("I hear you") → ask for context and isolate ("besides that, is there anything else?") → ask permission, then reframe to their own stated outcome and cost (price versus cost, time versus information, problem versus symptom, or a similar client's story) → re-close straight away (Sapp's AARR, Martell's steps). For "not now," a calm deframe and consequence questions rather than rebuttals (Miner). Bonus: the objection was pre-empted in discovery so it never needed handling. Score handling on calls with no objections at 4 if discovery visibly pre-empted them, otherwise "not observed."

**9. Talk ratio and listening.**
- 1: Rep over 70 percent talk in discovery; interrupts; questions pile up with no follow-up.
- 3: Rep 55 to 65 percent; some follow-ups; occasional stepping on answers.
- 5: Rep under 50 percent in discovery (Sapp targets 20 percent); follows up on the emotional word ("in what way though?"); bridges into the next question off the prospect's last words instead of filler ("okay, okay, gotcha") (Miner); pauses before responding; questions specific and spread across the call, not generic and front-loaded.

**10. Tonality and certainty.** Judge from the transcript as far as it allows (hedging words, filler, run-on sentences, apologizing for price). Say plainly when audio would be needed to judge this properly.
- 1: Hedging ("I mean, it's kind of, like, maybe..."), apologizing for price, rambling.
- 3: Mostly confident; wobbles at price or objections.
- 5: Calm, curious, certain: not monotone, not hyped, not timid (Miner); "high intent, low attachment"; short sentences that end in periods with downward inflection (Sapp); a concerned tone on consequence questions; states the price plainly and stops.

## Mechanics strip (measure on every call)

Report these as estimates from the transcript and say they're estimates:

- Rep talk share: overall, and discovery segment only
- Longest rep monologue (duration or word count) and where it happened
- Questions asked, and how they're distributed across the call
- Minute of the prospect's first real pain statement
- Minute of the first price mention
- Number of times the rep asked for the sale
- Objections raised (and classification)
- Transcript signals found (rep and prospect), with timestamps, from `transcript-signals.md`
- Outcome

## Ranking rules

1. Sort by call score.
2. Tie-break by outcome: closed paid in full > closed with payment plan > deposit > follow-up booked with a date > follow-up without date > lost.
3. Mark **false positives** (closed, score under 65) and **false negatives** (lost, score over 80) separately; give the one-line reason for each.
4. Rep rankings need at least five scored calls per rep; show the count next to every rep's average.
