# Closer Frameworks: The Source Material

This file distills what elite closers teach about running a sales call, then merges it into one master stage model that the scorecard grades against. Read the master model first; the individual frameworks are there so feedback can say "this is the Hormozi Overview step you skipped" and mean something specific.

## Provenance tags (read before quoting anyone)

Quotes in this file came from public transcripts and summaries of these people's videos and podcasts, gathered November 2026. They carry tags so the skill never claims more certainty than it has:

- **[V]** verbatim from a transcript of the person speaking (auto-captions, so minor wording errors are possible)
- **[S]** from an AI or editorial summary of their content: the idea is theirs, the wording may not be
- **[P]** third-party paraphrase or secondhand report
- **[G]** general, widely taught version of their method, not tied to one transcript; verify against their own material before quoting it as theirs

## The master stage model (what the scorecard grades)

Every high-ticket call framework below lands on the same skeleton. The names differ; the order doesn't.

| # | Stage | Job of the stage | Hormozi CLOSER | Miner NEPQ | Sapp | Martell |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frame and connect | Set the agenda, get permission to ask questions, lower the guard | (pre-C) | Connecting | Intro, first 10% | Opener question |
| 2 | Clarify the why | Why are they here, why now | Clarify | Situation | Current state | Where are you now |
| 3 | Dig the pain | What they've tried, why it failed, what it costs | Overview (pain cycle) | Problem awareness, consequence | Probing and provoking questions, cost of inaction | The gap (pain side) |
| 4 | Paint the outcome | Specific, emotional picture of after | (C's 12-month question) | Solution awareness | Future state, "magic wand" | The gap (promise side), one-year question |
| 5 | Label and confirm | Restate the problem back; prospect agrees it's the problem | Label | (transition) | Logic plus emotion recap | Commitment test (1 to 10) |
| 6 | Permission and pitch | Ask to present; present only what solves their stated pain | Sell the vacation | Presentation, in their words | Sell the sizzle | One offer, one outcome |
| 7 | Ask and handle | Ask for the sale clearly, then handle concerns | Explain away concerns | Committing questions | Four-step objection method | Five-step objection method |
| 8 | Lock and reinforce | Payment, next steps, prevent buyer's remorse | Reinforce | | Clear game plan | Follow-up ladder, delivery |

Rule that runs through all of it: **the prospect says the pain, not the rep.** Value in the buyer's mind is how well their problem was articulated back to them (Hormozi [S]). A rep who tells the prospect what their problem is has skipped the stage, however accurate they are.

## Alex Hormozi: CLOSER

Sources: "How I Built A 20+ Sales Team [The Process]" (caption transcript, rosetta.to); "I Taught 115+ Salesmen My Closing Framework" (Ep 305/306, deciphr.ai summary with excerpts); "How to Close a Deal on the Phone Before They Hang Up" (Ep 952, Shortform summary); "How to do a Sales Pitch" (sozai.app transcript).

**The core rule.** Everything in the script is a question. No paragraphs, because reps start reading them; questions make the prospect pause and answer, which makes the script scalable to any rep. [V paraphrased closely] Only three things should come out of a closer's mouth: questions, restatements, and short anecdotal stories. [V: "There's only three things that ever come out of your mouth. Questions, restatements, and anecdotal stories."]

**C, Clarify.** Clarify why the person is on the call. [V: "clarify why the person is there."] Example questions [V]: "what made you get on the call?" / "What's your goal?" / "Why is that important to you?" / "What would it look like 12 months from now if this ideal outcome were to be achieved?"

**L, Label.** Restate their problem and get agreement. [V: "So what I'm hearing is you've done X, Y, and Z, and the missing link has been X... Is that right?"] Once they confirm, the rep now has a problem to solve.

**O, Overview past pain.** [V: "So, what have you done in the past?"] then "Why did that not work for you?" Loop through every past attempt (the "pain cycle") until they've got nothing left, then recap the pain. The advantage the rep has: they're still on the phone with you, so nothing they tried has worked yet.

**S, Sell the vacation.** [V: "sell the vacation, not the plane."] Sell the outcome, not the mechanics of the program. Use roughly three short stories, about 30 seconds each, one per pillar of the solution. Simplicity scales with how much the buyer has to do: DIY and done-with-you offers should feel simple; done-for-you offers can show more steps. If there are too many steps, chunk them into bigger buckets. Feature-stacking and making it feel complicated are named failure modes. Ask permission before pitching: the prospect should say something like "walk me through it" first.

**E, Explain away concerns.** [V: "crazy people buy without obstacles. Normal people have concerns."] Objection responses must be drilled until automatic, with flashcards. Three buckets [V]: "It's price, it's stall, it's decisionmaker." Price means they don't see the value. Stalls ("think about it," "talk to my spouse") get drilled responses. Decision-maker: lean on agreements already made; the partner already knows they're struggling and looking for help. He also walks people through a chain of yes/no decisions (do you like me, do you like the product, do you think we can get you this outcome, do you have access to the money) then: [V: "What card do you want to use?"] Ask for the main concern directly and offer to think it through together [S].

**R, Reinforce.** Added years later because the sale continues through the whole customer relationship [S]. Right after the close, send something personal (founder video, handwritten card) to stop buyer's remorse. Onboarding is a second close.

**On tonality.** He teaches that words carry a small share of the message and how they're said carries most of it [S]. Treat that ratio as a teaching exaggeration, not a statistic.

**On managing and reviewing.** Record every call [V: "If you're not recording every single call that your people are on, one, you're not compliant."] Recording makes reps accountable to the script. In 1:1s, each rep brings their best, worst, and average call; the manager asks them to show where they clarified the problem, outlined the pain, asked for the sale, and overcame objections. [V: "You can never make a sale if you never ask for it."] Daily huddles, weekly 1:1s, monthly reviews, a published leaderboard, and regularly cutting the bottom performers.

**Mistakes he names:** statements instead of questions; explaining too much ("the more details they're going to be able to use" against you [V fragment]); asking for the sale before the rep is confident of a yes; feature stacking; not clarifying pain; never asking for the sale; un-drilled objections.

## Jeremy Miner: NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning)

Sources: Triple Session catalog pages for Miner sessions on consequence questions and precision probing [S]; 11x.ai guide [P, vendor page, treat cautiously]. Most NEPQ detail below is [G]: the widely taught version of the method. Primary source for exact wording is his "Black Book of Questions" and NEPQ training, which were not accessible for this build.

**Philosophy [G].** The rep is a problem finder, not a product pusher. The prospect persuades themselves through answering questions; the rep never tells, pushes, or argues. Objections are mostly prevented in discovery rather than handled at the end. Emotion drives the decision; logic justifies it.

**Stage sequence [G, six-stage version confirmed in P].** Connecting → Situation → Problem awareness → Solution awareness → Consequence → Commitment. The longer taught version adds qualifying, transition, and presentation between consequence and commitment.

- **Connecting:** lower the guard, shift attention from the rep to the prospect, ask why they took the call.
- **Situation:** what's going on now, what are they using, how long.
- **Problem awareness:** what isn't working, why, how long, what it's doing to them. Probes [S]: "In what way though?" / "What's that doing to you?" Also ask how long the problem has gone on, ask for a specific example, and mirror back the emotional word they used with a concerned tone.
- **Solution awareness:** what would change if it were solved, what that would mean to them personally.
- **Consequence:** what happens if nothing changes. Start the question with a challenging tone, soften to concern at the end so it doesn't feel like an attack [S]. A push-away question tests urgency [S]: "why look at doing this now, why not push it down the road?"
- **Commitment:** soft committing questions, e.g. "Would you be opposed to..." framing [P] and asking how they feel they should proceed [P].

**Delivery [S/G].** Neutral, curious, calm tonality: neither hyped enthusiasm nor timid uncertainty. Deliberate pauses get deeper answers; asking the next question too fast gets surface answers. Feeling and impact questions only land after trust and the gap are built; asked early, they backfire [S]. Use the prospect's own words, not vague labels like "problem," "situation," or "solution" [S]. Move from business impact down to personal impact [S].

**What NEPQ flags as wrong [G].** Pitching before the prospect has stated the problem and its consequence; enthusiasm that reads as pushy; logic-only selling; arguing with objections instead of asking questions about them.

## Shelby Sapp: logic plus emotion

Sources: "How to Sell Anything (Full Sales Course)" (sozai.app transcript, first section verbatim, rest summarized); Big Deal podcast #137 summary [P]; host breakdown of her "She Sells" program [P].

**Sell both halves of the brain.** Her coaching story about two failed reps [V]: "You sold too much with logic and zero emotion and you sold with only emotion and no logic." Every call needs the numbers and the feeling.

**Positive and negative data.** Quantify the gain and the cost of waiting. Her example: someone at $8K a month aiming for $20K loses $12K for every month of delay [V].

**Questions.** Future pacing [V]: "Where would you want to be in six months if everything worked out perfectly?" Then pressure-test the number [V]: "Why do you want to make 50K? Why not 49? Why not 51?" Three buckets of discovery questions: current state, desired state, cost of inaction [S]; another version names them probing, provoking, and future state [P].

**Structure [S].** Roughly 10 percent intro, 80 percent discovery (where objections get pre-empted), 10 percent close. Client talks about 80 percent of the time [P]. "Listen to understand, not to reply" [S].

**Sell the outcome.** [V: "They don't buy a Zoom call. They buy the accountability, the energy, and the live coaching..."] [V: "You need to sell the dream. Sell the vacation."]

**Objections [S].** Objections are limiting beliefs, not logic [P]. Four steps: acknowledge without agreeing; ask for context and isolate the real objection; answer with a reframe, analogy, or story (price versus cost, time versus information, problem versus symptom), asking permission before challenging a belief; redirect and re-close. Common list [P]: partner, too expensive, send me info, need time, competitor's cheaper, saw a bad review, already working with someone.

**Closing [S/P].** Get a non-money micro-commitment before price. Say "investment," not "cost." After the price, stay silent; the first person to speak loses [S]. Create urgency through clarity, not pressure [P]: a clear game plan makes yes easier than staying confused. Assumptive and option closes [S].

**Delivery.** [V: "Sales is 80% the energy that you bring into it and your mindset."] She found by reviewing her own recordings that she blended sentences with commas; speaking in periods, with downward inflection, raised her authority [P].

**Review practice.** Record your calls and review them [P]. In role-play, rate yourself 1 to 10 on script, tonality, body language, rapport, and energy, and get to 8+ before live calls [S].

**Mistakes she names [S unless marked].** Logic-only or emotion-only selling [V]; low, half-in energy [V]; skipping training after a win [V]; pitching before discovery; fake compliments; letting the prospect rant with no direction; filling the silence after price; overselling after a yes; raising objections the prospect didn't have; accepting unrealistic goals without challenge.

## Dan Martell: the gap

Source: "How to Make $100K in 6 Weeks With a Productized Service" (danmartell.com, article verbatim plus video transcript). Note: this material is built around chat/DM selling; it transfers to calls, but say so when applying it.

**The gap.** [V: "The whole point of a sale, it only happens in the gap."] Pain today on one side, the promised outcome on the other. No gap, no sale.

**Opening and qualifying [V].** Open with a qualifying question ("Are you here for the content or are you looking to grow your business?"), then where they are now, their one-year goals, and a forward question ("imagine we were getting together in one year...").

**Commitment test [V].** "On a scale of 1 to 10, is this a now thing or a later thing?" Then quantify the cost of not changing.

**One offer.** [V: "One page. One offer. One outcome."] Two price options: a high anchor and the one you want sold. [V: "A confused mind never buys."]

**Objection framework [V, article].**
1. Acknowledge: "Makes sense. I would ask the same."
2. Isolate: "Besides that, is there anything else in the way?"
3. Clarify what they really mean, then reframe to the outcome they said they wanted.
4. Two paths: stay the same, or invest and make it back.
5. Close with a question: "Want me to drop the link?"

**Closing.** [V: "Silence closes deals. Let them think and respond."] Follow up while it's hot: 30 minutes, 60 minutes, next morning, 24 hours, 3 days, 7 days. [V: "Fortunes are made in the follow-up."]

**Mistakes he names [V].** "Your inability to want to feel pushy is stopping you from serving people." The biggest issue is the rep's inability to ask for the deal. Debating what the prospect means instead of asking [S].

## Gong: what the data says

Source: Gong Labs, "Mastering the talk-to-listen ratio in sales calls" (2025 analysis of 326K calls of 10+ minutes, plus the 2016 study of 25K calls). Gong sells call-recording software and the data is correlational, B2B-heavy, and averaged across industries. Use it as a benchmark, not a law.

- Average rep talk share is about 60 percent. Won deals average around 57 percent; lost deals around 62 percent. Low performers swing from 54 percent (won) to 64 percent (lost); top performers hold steady.
- 2016 study: the highest-converting calls sat near 43 percent talk, and talking more than 65 percent of the call tracked with lower conversion.
- Questions: won deals averaged 15 to 16 questions per call; lost deals about 20. More questions isn't better; interrogation hurts. Top reps spread questions evenly through the call; average reps front-load them.
- Long seller monologues show up in lost deals.
- Pause about two seconds after the prospect stops talking before responding.
- Restatement test: "So what I'm hearing is X is your top concern. Does that sound right?"

For one-call-close consumer offers, expect slightly higher rep talk share during the pitch segment than Gong's B2B averages. Grade the discovery segment against the Gong numbers and the pitch segment separately.
