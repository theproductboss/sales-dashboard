# Transcript Signals

Patterns to scan for in every transcript before scoring. Each one is something you can see in the text, so feedback can quote it directly. Count them, note the timestamps, and feed them into the scorecard dimension listed. Speaker labels from transcription tools are often wrong; fix them before counting.

## Rep signals (things the rep says)

| Signal | Looks like | Why it matters | Dimension |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rapport before purpose** | Opens with small talk and compliments before saying why they're on the call | Reads like a setup for an ask and triggers resistance (Sapp) | 1 |
| **No permission frame** | Starts asking questions with no "is it okay if I ask a few questions to see if we can help?" | No doctor frame; rep gets pushed around later (Martell) | 1 |
| **Generic discovery questions** | "What are your biggest challenges?" "What keeps you up at night?" | Predictable, gets surface answers (Miner) | 2, 3 |
| **Moving on after a pain point** | Prospect names a pain; rep's next line is an unrelated scripted question | No conversational threading (Sapp); pain stays shallow | 3 |
| **One-layer consequence** | Asks "what happens if you don't fix this?" once and accepts the first answer | Miner goes six layers; the sale is made in the deeper layers | 3 |
| **Filler after answers** | "Okay, okay. Gotcha, gotcha. I'm curious, let me ask you..." | Sounds scripted; rep is planning the next question, not listening (Miner) | 9 |
| **Parroting** | Repeats back everything the prospect said, every answer | Sounds like a salesperson (Miner). One clean label of the core problem is good; constant paraphrase isn't | 5, 9 |
| **Rapid-fire questions** | Several questions in a row with short answers between, no pauses | Kneejerk answers (Miner, verbal pacing) | 9 |
| **Pitching at the first pain** | "Well, that's exactly why you need our program..." in discovery | Spends the ammo early and puts the guard back up (Sapp) | 6 |
| **Feature dump** | A list of deliverables with no link to a pain the prospect named | Selling the plane, not the vacation (Hormozi) | 6 |
| **Free consulting** | Rep solves the problem on the call | Releases the pressure, so nothing to buy (Martell) | 6 |
| **Hedging** | "kind of," "sort of," "maybe," "I think," "just wanted to," "does that make sense?" after every point | Uncertainty; people don't follow uncertain people (Miner) | 10 |
| **Commas, not periods** | Long run-on sentences joined with "and," "so," "um" | Weak authority (Sapp) | 10 |
| **Apologizing for price** | "I know it's a lot, but..." "Don't worry, there are payment plans" before any objection | Creates the price objection | 7, 10 |
| **"Cost" or "price" instead of "investment"** | "The cost is..." | Small, but Sapp's rule; note it, don't over-weight it | 7 |
| **Talking after the price** | Rep speaks again within a few seconds of saying the number | First person to speak after price loses (Sapp) | 7 |
| **Vague or no ask** | "So, yeah, let me know what you think" / "Are you ready to move forward?" | No clear ask, or a yes/no ask instead of this-or-that (Martell) | 7 |
| **Overselling after a yes** | Adds more features or bonuses after the prospect agreed | Creates doubt; go straight to paperwork (Sapp) | 7 |
| **Agreeing with the objection** | "Yeah, it is expensive." "Totally, everyone says that." | Reinforces the limiting belief (Sapp) | 8 |
| **Rebuttal stacking** | Three counter-arguments in a row | Me versus you; no isolation, no question | 8 |
| **Discount before isolation** | Offers a lower price before finding the real objection | Trains buyers to object; often hides the real issue | 8 |
| **Accepting the stall** | "No problem, I'll send you some info" with no time booked | No book-a-meeting-from-a-meeting (Martell) | 8 |
| **Silent paperwork** | Long gaps while taking payment | Buyer talks themselves out of it (Sapp) | 7 |
| **Follow-up trigger words** | "Just following up," "just circling back," "just checking in" | Salesperson bucket (Miner, Martell) | follow-up calls |
| **Qualifying to the prospect** | "Sure, I'll call you whenever works!" chasing a lead's schedule | Lowers status, leads to being ghosted (Miner) | 1 |

## Prospect signals (things the prospect says)

| Signal | Looks like | What it predicts | What the rep should have done |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-committal language** | "If I could at least...", "we're not doing that bad," "I'd be okay with," a small goal | A smokescreen objection later: think about it, not now, I can do it myself (Miner's open loops) | Challenge the word and re-anchor the goal in discovery |
| **Cost-based thinking** | Mentions things being too expensive, buying fakes, waiting for deals | Price objection (Sapp) | Pre-handle: "Are you at a place to make a real investment in solving this?" |
| **"We" / partner references** | "My husband and I," "we usually decide together" | Spouse objection (Sapp) | Ask how past similar decisions were made, and how the partner feels about the problem (Miner) |
| **Old problem** | "This has been going on for three years" | "I'll think about it" (Sapp) | "Why didn't you solve it then? Why now?" Get them to say why now |
| **Past failures** | "I tried X and it didn't work" | Fit/belief objection | Hormozi's pain cycle: why didn't it work? Then a story about someone similar |
| **Unicorn goal** | An unrealistic target | Low certainty, so no buy, or a refund later (Sapp) | Anchor to what's typical; build certainty |
| **Surface answers** | One-word or vague replies | Shallow pain, guard still up | Camouflaged, specific questions; verbal pacing; follow-ups |
| **Emotional words** | "frustrated," "embarrassed," "scared," "exhausted" | An opening to go deeper | Mirror the word with concern; "in what way though?" |
| **Clarifying question after price** | "Does that include the one-on-ones?" | Usually a buying signal, not an objection (Sapp) | Answer it, ask if there's anything else, go to paperwork |
| **Their own "why now"** | "Revenue dropped and my team said it's time" | A strong anchor for the close | Bank it; use it at the objection |

## How to use this file

- Run the scan on step 1 of the review and list the signals found, with timestamps, in the mechanics strip.
- A signal is evidence, not a verdict. One "kind of" isn't hedging; a pattern is.
- The prospect signals are where most of the diagnosis value is: if a prospect signal appeared and the rep didn't act on it, that moment is often the turning point of the call.
- When the house playbook adds its own signals (product-specific red-flag words), they go in the playbook amendments and get scanned for too.
