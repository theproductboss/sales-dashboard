# Feedback Format

The report is written for the rep first and the manager second. Lead with what to do differently tomorrow, back every point with a quote, and keep it short enough to read before the next call.

## Single-call review template

```
CALL REVIEW: [Rep] × [Prospect first name or initials] · [date] · [call type]
Outcome: [closed PIF / plan / deposit / follow-up booked / lost / disqualified]
Score: [NN]/100 ([grade])   Flags: [any automatic flags, or "none"]

MECHANICS
Talk share [NN%] (discovery [NN%]) · Longest monologue [m:ss at mm:ss]
Questions [N], [spread / front-loaded] · First pain statement [mm:ss]
First price mention [mm:ss] · Asked for sale [N]x · Objections: [types]

THE MOMENT
[mm:ss] "[the quoted line where the call was won or started dying]"
[2 to 3 sentences: why this was the turning point, and the mechanism.]

KEEP DOING
1. [Behavior] — [mm:ss] "[quote]" — [why it worked, naming the framework step]
2. ...
3. ...

FIX
1. [Behavior] — [mm:ss] "[quote]" — [what it cost the call]
   Instead: "[rewrite in the rep's voice]"
2. ...
3. ...

OBJECTION DIAGNOSIS
[Objection] at [mm:ss] · Type: [type] · Handle: [grade 1-5 + one line]
Root cause: [earlier stage + quoted evidence of the skip]

SCORECARD
[table: dimension · score · weight · one-line evidence]

YOUR DRILL BEFORE THE NEXT CALL
[One drill. What to practice, how many reps, what "done" looks like.]

PATTERN (from your last [N] calls)
[One or two lines on the recurring pattern, or "first call logged" if none.]
```

## Writing rules

- **Every point has a quote and a timestamp.** If you can't quote it, you can't claim it.
- **Specific beats general.** Not "build more rapport." Instead: "At 3:10 she mentioned her daughter's recital and you went straight to your next question. Ask one follow-up about it."
- **Keep and fix are three each, maximum.** If there are more problems, they're in the scorecard. The rep only needs the three that matter most.
- **Rewrites are questions, restatements, or short stories.** Never a paragraph for the rep to read aloud.
- **Name the framework step, lightly.** "You skipped the Overview step (what they've tried and why it failed)" helps. A lecture on Hormozi doesn't.
- **On the rep's side.** Direct, never contemptuous. The goal is a better next call, not a verdict on the person.
- **Don't over-claim tonality from text.** Say "from the transcript, it reads as hedging" and suggest checking the audio.

```
[Rep] · avg [NN] over [N] calls · close rate [NN%] · strongest [dimension] · weakest [dimension] · current drill

BEST CLIPS TO STUDY
- [stage]: [call] [mm:ss] "[quote]"
```

## Drill library

Pick the one that matches the biggest leak.

- **Pain cycle reps.** Role-play with a partner playing a prospect; ask "what have you tried?" and "why didn't that work?" until they run out. Done when you can go 4+ loops without pitching.
- **Label drill.** After every role-play, restate the prospect's problem in their exact words and get a "yes, exactly." Done when 3 in a row get that response.
- **Shut-up drill.** Say the price, then count silently to five. Done when you can do it 10 times in role-play without speaking first. (Sapp: the first person who talks after the price loses.)
- **Ask-for-it drill.** End every practice call with a direct ask. Done when the ask is one sentence with no hedge words.
- **Objection flashcards.** One card per objection type; partner flips, rep answers with acknowledge-isolate-clarify-reframe-close. Done at under 3 seconds to start each answer (Hormozi's flashcard drilling).
- **Question-only discovery.** Run a 10-minute discovery where every rep sentence must end in a question mark. Done when you do it without a statement slipping in.
- **Six layers deep (Miner).** Partner names a surface problem; keep asking "and what happens then?" until you reach personal or family impact. Done when you reach 5+ layers without it sounding like an interrogation.
- **Bridge, don't fill (Miner).** Record a role-play and count every "okay, okay," "gotcha," "I'm curious, let me ask you." Redo it, launching each next question off the prospect's last words. Done at zero fillers.
- **Camouflage the question (Miner).** Rewrite your five most generic discovery questions into specific "what's caused you to feel like..." versions that use this offer's language. Done when none of them could be asked on any other company's call.
- **Thread it (Sapp).** Partner gives one pain point; ask 8 follow-ups off that one point before moving on. Done when you can do it without repeating a question.
- **AARR reps (Sapp).** Flashcards of the six common objections; answer each with acknowledge, ask, answer, redirect, ending in a re-close question. Done when every answer ends with a question.
- **Pre-handle scan (Sapp, Martell).** Re-read your last three transcripts and mark every partner, cost, and "old problem" signal you didn't follow up. Write the pre-handle question you should have asked for each.
- **Periods, not commas.** Record yourself pitching, then cut every run-on into short sentences that end flat (Sapp). Re-record.
- **Self-review.** Before reading this skill's score, rate your own recorded call out of 10 on Sapp's five: script, tonality, body language, connection or rapport, energy. Then compare. Big gaps between your rating and the score are the coaching conversation.
